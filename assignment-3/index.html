Where mascots came from

Published 21 October 2026

A mascot is a person, animal, or object believed to bring good luck, or a character that represents a team, school, or brand. Today the word usually means a costumed character cheering on a sports team.

A lucky charm on stage

The English word comes from the French mascotte, meaning a charm or talisman. It became popular after La Mascotte, a comic opera by Edmond Audran that opened in Paris in 1880. In the story, a farm girl brings good fortune to everyone around her, and the word quickly spread into English.

Live animals on the sidelines

The first sports mascots were often real animals. Yale University's bulldog, Handsome Dan, first appeared in 1889 and is widely called the first live mascot in American college sports. Others followed, including the University of Georgia's bulldog Uga in 1956 and the University of Colorado's buffalo Ralphie in 1966, who still runs onto the field before home football games with a team of handlers.

The costumed mascot arrives

Costumed characters became common in the 1960s and 1970s. The New York Mets introduced Mr. Met, a man with a baseball for a head, in 1964. In 1974 Ted Giannoulas put on a chicken suit for a San Diego radio station and became the San Diego Chicken, one of the first mascots famous for comedy instead of just cheering.

The biggest change came in 1978 with the Phillie Phanatic, a big green creature designed by Bonnie Erickson and Wayde Harrison, who had worked on the Muppets. The Phanatic teased umpires, danced on dugouts, and showed teams that a mascot could be part of the entertainment.

Mascot words to know

Live mascot
A real animal that represents a team, cared for by trained handlers.
Costumed mascot
A performer in a full suit who stays in character and usually does not speak.
Corporate mascot
A character that represents a company or product, such as the Michelin Man, first drawn in 1898.
NCAA
The body that runs most American college sports, where mascot traditions are strongest.

lightbulb Did you know?

There is a Mascot Hall of Fame. It was founded by David Raymond, the original performer inside the Phillie Phanatic, and opened a museum in Whiting, Indiana, in 2018.
