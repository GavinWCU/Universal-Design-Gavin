Why

Build for everyone

You wouldn't build a stadium with one entrance and no ramps. So why ship a website that only works one way?

On this page

  1. It's a lot of people
  2. It helps everyone
  3. It's the law
  4. It makes better sites
  5. It drives innovation
  6. It's our turn

It's a lot of people

  • The CDC says more than 1 in 4 U.S. adults has some type of disability. Most common: cognition, mobility, hearing, vision.
  • The number grows as the population ages. Ability is a spectrum, not a yes-or-no.
  • Chip link: closing every fourth stadium gate would never be called a small problem.

It helps everyone

  • The curb-cut effect: captions, high contrast and keyboard access help everyone in loud rooms, bright sun, or a hurry.
  • Disabilities can be permanent, temporary or situational.

It's the law

  • ADA and Section 508.
  • The 2024 Department of Justice rule says public universities like CU must meet WCAG 2.1 AA. The first deadline is now April 2027.
  • Employers want designers who already know accessibility.

It makes better sites

  • Better SEO, cleaner code that is easier to maintain, and fewer confused users.

It drives innovation

  • Haben Girma, the first Deafblind Harvard Law graduate: disabled people drive innovation.
  • Audiobooks, captions and text-to-speech started as access tech.

It's our turn

  • Tim Berners-Lee: the power of the web is in its universality.
  • Web Standards, CSS Zen Garden and HTML5 came from designers and developers demanding change. Universal design is next.
